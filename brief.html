Casualty Insurers Face $100 Billion Reserve Correction As Analytics Lag: Moody's

What happened Moody's reports that the U.S. casualty market is allowing adverse reserve development to accumulate largely undetected until it surfaces in financial statements, and sizes the outstanding correction in the region of $100 billion. The finding is framed as an analytics and detection problem rather than a single-year pricing miss.
Mentioned Moody's; U.S. casualty market
Why it matters Reserve adequacy drives earnings quality, capital position and rating agency treatment. If deterioration is only visible once it reaches the statements, corrections arrive as earnings events rather than managed true-ups — which changes how casualty rate need, reinsurance structure and reserving investment should be argued internally.

What to watch

Third-quarter and full-year 2026 statutory reserve development disclosures, and whether casualty rate filings or reinsurance attachment points move ahead of the January 1 renewal.

02

First-Half U.S. P/C Industry Underwriting Gain Jumps to $31.7B

What happened The U.S. property/casualty industry posted a net underwriting gain of $31.7 billion for the first half of 2026, up from $11.6 billion in the prior-year period, according to a report from Verisk and the American Property Casualty Insurance Association (APCIA).
Mentioned Verisk; American Property Casualty Insurance Association (APCIA); U.S. property/casualty industry
Why it matters A near-tripling of first-half underwriting profit rebuilds capital and historically precedes competitive pressure on rate. Read alongside the reserve-development warning above, it sets up a familiar tension: strong current-accident-year results funding growth while prior-year adequacy remains unresolved.

What to watch

Whether the second half holds, given that the first-half improvement leaned on lower catastrophe losses against the prior year's Los Angeles wildfires, and how rate filings respond into 2027.

03

Cat Bond Issuance Hits Record High As Reinsurance Capital Floods Market

What happened Catastrophe bond issuance reached a record high, and abundant capital pushed reinsurance and insurance-linked securities pricing lower at the mid-year renewals, according to AM Best.
Mentioned AM Best; catastrophe bond and ILS markets
Why it matters Record alternative capacity alongside lower mid-year pricing confirms the supply side is not the constraint heading into January 1. Cedents gain negotiating room on price and structure; reinsurers and ILS managers are pushed to defend margin through attachment point, terms and portfolio selection rather than rate.

What to watch

Commentary out of the Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous on whether discipline holds on attachment points and terms, and fourth-quarter cat bond issuance and spread levels.

04

Gallagher Re Estimates €2.5B in August Western Europe Storm and Hail Losses

What happened Severe convective storm outbreaks across Western Europe from 24–28 August are expected to approach €2.5 billion in industry losses, according to Gallagher Re. Storm Quiria produced large hail, damaging winds and tornadoes in western France from 24–26 August; storm Raphaela drove hail-led losses across France, Switzerland and Italy on 27–28 August. Gallagher Re splits the estimate at €0.75–1.25 billion in Switzerland, roughly €0.5–1 billion in Italy and €0.5–0.75 billion in France, with damage to residential and commercial property, motor, agribusiness and solar installations.
Mentioned Gallagher Re; Switzerland; Italy; France; storms Quiria and Raphaela
Why it matters A multi-billion-euro secondary-peril event lands directly into the January 1 European renewal conversation, at a moment when abundant capacity is pressuring price. Severe convective storm remains the peril where attachment points and aggregate covers are argued hardest, and solar and agribusiness exposure continues to show up in hail losses.

What to watch

Revisions to the industry estimate as claims develop, and whether European property programmes see movement on SCS attachment points or aggregate terms at January 1.

05

EQT Agrees to Acquire Majority Stake in McGill and Partners for $2B from Warburg Pincus

What happened EQT X has entered a definitive agreement to acquire a majority stake in specialty broker McGill and Partners from Warburg Pincus for $2 billion. Founded in May 2019 by Steve McGill with John Lloyd, Stephen Cross and Karl Hennessy, the firm now reports revenues in excess of $250 million, more than 600 colleagues across seven countries and over 1,000 clients. McGill continues as CEO and Lloyd remains chairman, both retaining significant stakes; Warburg Pincus exits in full. EQT has committed to a new Equity Participation Plan open to every colleague.
Mentioned EQT; McGill and Partners; Warburg Pincus; Steve McGill; John Lloyd
Why it matters A $2 billion valuation on a seven-year-old specialty broker is a visible benchmark for independent broking assets and confirms continued private-capital appetite for distribution. Because this is a sponsor change rather than absorption into a larger broker, the firm stays in the market as an independent competitor for specialty talent and accounts.

What to watch

Whether the new sponsor funds acquisitions or accelerated hiring, and how the colleague equity plan affects producer retention against competing offers.

06

QBE Completes Sale of Global Trade Credit and Surety Unit to Swiss Re Corporate Solutions

What happened QBE Insurance Group has completed the sale of its Global Trade Credit and Surety operations to Swiss Re Corporate Solutions. The transaction, announced in February 2026, transfers operations across Australia, New Zealand and the UK — representing approximately $200 million in annual revenues — along with the underwriting team. Swiss Re cites economic uncertainty, more complex supply chains and rising demand for risk transfer in a global segment it sizes at around $19 billion in annual premium.
Mentioned QBE Insurance Group; Swiss Re Corporate Solutions; Australia; New Zealand; United Kingdom
Why it matters This is completed, not announced — capacity and underwriting talent have moved. QBE reallocates capital toward its stated growth lines, while Swiss Re Corporate Solutions deepens a credit and surety practice at a point in the cycle when trade credit demand is being driven by supply-chain and macro uncertainty. Brokers placing credit and surety in these three markets should expect a changed capacity map.

What to watch

Retention of the transferred underwriting team, and whether QBE names the growth lines receiving the reallocated capital at its next results.

07

Guy Carpenter Rebranded as Marsh Re

What happened Guy Carpenter, the reinsurance arm of Marsh, has been rebranded Marsh Re, retiring a name that has been in the insurance and reinsurance market for more than a century. The announcement positions Marsh Re as bringing together reinsurance and capital solutions under an expanded remit.
Mentioned Guy Carpenter; Marsh; Marsh Re
Why it matters Retiring a hundred-year-old intermediary brand in favour of the parent signals a tighter integration of reinsurance broking with Marsh's wider capital and advisory offering. For cedents, the practical question is whether the combined proposition changes how reinsurance and capital markets solutions are presented at renewal — and whether competitors position against it.

What to watch

How the rebrand is received at the Monte Carlo Rendez-Vous, and any accompanying changes to leadership or service structure ahead of January 1.
